# Ideas

- 2026-11-10 · Locked-cursor "d-pad" mode for mouse/trackpad: lock the pointer, slide left/right to pick a tray piece, hold and move to push it relatively, and the next piece is selected at once after each drop, so you never drag the cursor back down. **Take:** strong; it removes the most tiring part of mouse play. The browser side is easy (Pointer Lock API). The Mac app is the risk: it needs UIKit pointer lock plus GameController mouse deltas, which haven't been tried in an iPad-on-Mac app. Esc must always free the pointer.
- 2026-11-25 · **v2: timer grace window.** Matt: "there's moments where I drop a block like 1ms after the timer runs out... I think there should be a TINY bit more leeway in v2." A drop that lands just after the hand timer hits zero still counts: a hidden grace window of ~100–250 ms, kept as a `tweaks.json` knob. Not a hotfix: it changes scoring, so it ships in v2 together with the v2 leaderboard reset (rule in AG_CONTEXT.md). **Take:** strong and cheap; this is standard "coyote time" in action games. The timer bar should still look empty at zero, so the grace stays invisible. Put the check where game over fires, so a drop that started before zero and lands inside the window is accepted.
- 2026-11-26 · **Chill as its own mode; start new players on it, then invite them to "Blitz".** Matt: "game chill mode should be its own game mode. It shouldn't be buried in settings. And I think players should be started on chill mode. And then after they've played for a bit, something should say, hey, do you want an extra challenge? Try the real game. And then we should have some name for it, like Blitz mode or something." **Take:** strong. Chill already keeps its own best score (`chill_best_score`), so the leaderboard needs no reset for this. Needs a mode picker on the start screen (designer first), a "played enough" trigger (e.g. 3 finished games or 10 minutes), and the invite shown once, with Blitz always one tap away after that.
- 2026-11-26 · **Sensitivity nudge.** Matt: sensitivity 1 is only there so a first drag tracks the finger exactly; "as soon as somebody actually figures out how to play the game and is getting somewhat comfortable, we need to increase the sensitivity for them, but in a way where they're aware of it". A one-time card with a slider set to a recommended value, which also points at Settings for later. **Take:** strong, and it pairs well with the Blitz invite (same moment, same card style; don't show both at once). Push-back on the wording "for noobs": friendlier copy like "Pros play faster. Try a higher sensitivity" keeps it from feeling like an insult.
- 2026-11-26 · **Name: "Don Blocko".** Matt: "I actually love 'Don Blocko:' as the first part." Other ideas he floated: Blocko, Blocko Homo (Jocko Homo nod, "probably not what one should name an app"), Merry Gridmas, Blocked Up, "Don Blocko: to face the Grid". Wants the name to have personality. **Take:** strong. "Don Blocko" has no App Store match (CA + US, checked 2026-11-26); plain "Blocko" is taken by several block puzzles (BLOCKO Block Puzzle Blast, Blocko Loco, Blocko.), so the "Don" is what makes it ownable. "Don Blocko" fits a Home Screen label uncut; "To Face the Grid" works as the store subtitle (16 chars). Change the display name and App Store Connect name only; the bundle ID stays. Trademark search (CIPO/USPTO) still not done.
- 2026-11-26 · **Story: the game takes place in the mind of Don Blocko, mid-existential crisis.** Matt: his wife of 20 years is divorcing him and taking him for all he's worth; in a litigation meeting he watches her play a falling-block game while her expensive lawyer explains she gets the dream home they designed and built together, to raise their daughter, whom Don can only see at her discretion. Eventually cinematics; the link between story and gameplay is meant to be absurdly flimsy. **Take:** strong and funny; it gives the "life or death" tone and the 10-second timer a reason (his panic). Two cautions: never name or show Tetris (its owners enforce the trademark hard), keep it "a block game on her phone"; and the divorce/custody material needs a light, absurd touch so it reads as comedy, not misery. Start tiny: one title-card line and Don's face reacting, before any cinematics.
- 2026-11-26 · **Story twist: she's playing a lame ad-stuffed block puzzle, and Don's fever dream is that beating her high score wins her back.** Matt: "What if his wife was playing one of the many Blockdoku games on the app store (we'll make it a lame version with ads and stuff), and he has this mental breakdown and has this weird fever dream where he imagines she just wants him to beat her high score, and that will win over her affection and restore the life he once loved." **Take:** better than the Tetris version. It fixes the trademark problem, and it hands the game its goal: her high score is the number Don chases, so the leaderboard *is* the plot. The fake lame app (banner ads, "WATCH AD TO CONTINUE") is a great joke and a quiet dig at the genre, but make it our own generic knock-off, never a copy of a real app's name or look. Cheap first step: her score shows as a ghost target in the run ("Linda's best: 4,210"), and the intro is one title card.
